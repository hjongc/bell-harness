# Business Document Design

## Start with the reader's decision

- Identify the recipient, what they need to decide, their reading conditions, the requested format, and the evidence available to share. Inspect the supplied copy, examples, and brand assets. For a layout-only revision, preserve the approved wording and content.
- Make the first page answer what this is, why it matters to this reader, and what to do or read next. For a short document, put those answers on the first content page. Give a separate cover or contents page room only if it helps orientation.
- Outline the pages using the actual material. Give each page or spread a question to answer, the evidence that answers it, and a clear reading order. Use headings that name the subject or finding. Cut generic claims and repeated headings before reducing type size.

## Choose a structure

| Document | Reading sequence |
|---|---|
| Portfolio or case study | Lead with the kind of work and the strongest relevant example. For each case, show the problem and constraints, personal role and collaborators, one consequential decision and why it was made, a labeled artifact, and the supported result. Distinguish personal contributions from team outcomes. If no outcome was measured, show documented feedback, an observed result, or the delivered work and say what the evidence establishes. |
| Proposal | Open with the client's goal and the decision requested. Follow with the approach, deliverables, scope inclusions and exclusions, milestones and responsibilities, price assumptions, and the next action. Identify unconfirmed terms and the conditions behind cost or schedule. |
| Company profile | Explain who the organization serves, what it delivers, and where it has done so. Pair capabilities with verifiable examples. End with a concrete next step and the supplied contact information. |
| Executive report | Lead with the finding and decision or action requested. Present the evidence, trade-offs, and uncertainties where they affect the decision. Put supporting methods, detailed tables, and references in an appendix when they interrupt the main argument. |

Use only supported metrics, testimonials, logos, and client claims. When an artifact is confidential or permission to share it is unclear, use an approved substitute, an irreversibly redacted asset, or a clearly labeled reconstruction with synthetic data. Check the export for recoverable redactions, hidden comments, tracked changes and unintended identifiers as applicable.

## Compose the pages

1. Set page size and orientation from the brief and delivery channel. Use the supplied system or choose a consistent margin grid, body column width, hierarchy of text styles, and caption and folio positions. Start with a continuous reading column for prose. Add a second column when it gives evidence or comparisons a clear relationship.
2. Test the hierarchy with the longest actual title, paragraph, caption, and table row. Align related labels and values, use stable spaces between groups, and keep long-form text at a comfortable line length. For Korean documents, use fonts with complete Hangul coverage, inspect mixed Hangul and Latin wrapping, and check punctuation, names, and units at reading size.
3. Use one strong piece of evidence where several decorative assets would compete. Crop case-study screenshots around the decision being described and caption what the reader should notice. Keep essential information in selectable text. Label charts with period, units, and source. State their takeaway in the surrounding text.
4. Style headings, lists, and tables semantically. Use data tables for comparisons, align comparable numbers, label units, repeat column headers across pages, and keep captions with figures. Use meaningful link text, legible contrast, and text alternatives for meaningful images when supported.

## Review the exported deliverable

- Use the available authoring and rendering workflow for the requested medium. Compose editable Word or Google Docs files with their paragraph and table styles. In slides, use the deck's masters and provide a separately inspected PDF when a handout is requested. For web print, check print styles and pagination in the intended export.
- Inspect the exact file the reader will receive. View every page first as thumbnails for sequence, rhythm and stray blank pages, then at reading size for fit, clipping, isolated headings, font substitution, figure clarity, and broken table rows. Open and test links and check reading order, searchable text, and accessible structure where tooling permits. For print, use the specified paper size and inspect the print preview.
- Correct defects in the editable source, then export again and recheck the affected pages and any subsequent pagination. Report the delivered formats, what was checked, and any export checks that could not run.
